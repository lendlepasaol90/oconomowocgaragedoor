What Custom Means

Doors Made for Your Opening and Your Home

A custom garage door is any door built to specifications that go beyond the standard sizes and designs on a dealer's stock list. That can mean a width or height that does not match the usual openings, a material like real cedar or mahogany, a window layout drawn for your house, a curved top section, or a color matched to your trim. Some custom doors are custom in one way only, such as an off-size steel door for an old garage. Others are fully designed pieces built to match a specific home.

Custom work starts with careful measuring. We record the exact width and height of the opening, the headroom above it, the side room on each wall, the depth of the garage and the condition of the framing. Older garages are rarely square, and a custom door has to fit the opening as it really is, not as the drawings say it should be. We also look at how the door will be lifted. Heavy wood doors and very tall doors need springs, tracks and openers sized for the job, and planning those parts at the start is what makes a custom door run smoothly.

Lead times for custom doors are longer than for stock doors because the sections are built to order. We explain the timeline when we quote, and if your current door is failing in the meantime, we can make repairs to keep it working until the new one arrives. We also stay in touch while the door is being built, so you know where things stand. When the door arrives, we schedule installation at a time that works for you and confirm the opening is ready before our crew shows up.

Tall Doors

Tall Garage Doors for Boats, RVs and Lake Life

Lake life in Oconomowoc means a lot of homeowners own something taller than a car. Pontoon boats on trailers, wake boats with towers, campers, travel trailers and lifted trucks all need more clearance than a standard seven-foot door gives. Tall doors of eight, ten, twelve feet or more let you park these inside, out of the snow and salt, instead of paying for storage or leaving them under a tarp all winter. Many newer homes at Pabst Farms already have one taller bay, and some older lake properties have added a dedicated boat garage.

A tall door is not just a stretched version of a short one. The sections are heavier, so springs and cables are sized differently. The tracks may need a high lift or vertical lift setup so the door rises along the wall and leaves room overhead for a car lift or storage. Wind load is a bigger factor on a tall, wide door, and reinforcing struts keep the sections from bowing. The opener must be matched to the door's weight and height, and a wall-mounted jackshaft opener is often the best fit for high lift tracks because it frees up the ceiling.

If you are adding a tall door to an existing garage, the framing and header may need work. We tell you what we see during the visit so you can plan with your builder or carpenter if structural changes are needed. Planning these details early avoids costly surprises. It also lets us size the springs, tracks and opener correctly the first time, so the door is safe and balanced from the day it goes in. Some owners also want a service door beside the tall bay, or a matching standard door on the next bay so the front of the garage looks balanced. We can plan those together.

  • Doors sized for pontoons, wake boats and campers
  • High lift and vertical lift track options
  • Reinforcing struts for wide, tall doors
  • Springs and cables matched to heavier sections
  • Wall-mount openers that clear the ceiling
  • Room for car lifts and overhead storage

Materials and Finishes

Real Wood, Custom Glass And Colors You Choose

Real wood doors are the heart of many custom projects. Cedar is light, stable and naturally resistant to rot, and it takes stain beautifully. Mahogany has a deep, rich color and tight grain that suits formal estate homes. Other species are available depending on the builder, and each has its own look and upkeep needs. Wood doors need a quality finish and regular resealing, especially here, where humid summers, snow and road salt work against them. We are upfront about that maintenance so you can decide with clear eyes. Some homeowners refinish every few years.

Custom glass can turn a garage door into a design feature. Options include clear, frosted, seeded, tinted and insulated glass, and window layouts can be drawn to echo the windows on the house, such as divided lites that match a historic front facade. Full-view aluminum and glass doors can be built in custom sizes for modern homes or lakeside rooms that double as a porch. Colors can be matched to your trim, shutters or front door, and steel and composite doors can be finished in custom paint for a precise match. Arched tops, where the top section follows a curved opening, are another custom touch that suits older brick and stone homes.

  • Cedar, mahogany and other wood species
  • Divided lite and seeded glass
  • Insulated glass for heated garages
  • Custom paint colors and stains
  • Arched and curved top sections
  • Decorative handles and hinges

What To Look For

Signs a Custom Door Is the Right Choice

Get A Free Quote

Your Opening Is Not Standard

Older garages and carriage houses around Oconomowoc often have openings that are wider, shorter or taller than stock sizes. If a standard door would need large filler boards or framing changes to fit, a custom size is usually the cleaner answer.

Your Boat Will Not Fit

If your pontoon, wake boat or camper lives outside all winter because the garage door is too short, a taller custom door can bring it inside, out of the snow, ice and salt that shorten its life.

The House Has Historic Character

A lake estate or a historic downtown home with a plain modern door looks unfinished. A custom carriage door designed from the home's own details brings the garage back in line with the rest of the property.

You Want Real Wood

Stock options are mostly steel and composite. If you want the warmth and grain of real cedar or mahogany, custom ordering is usually how you get it, sized and styled to your home. Real wood also takes custom stain.

The Opening Is Arched

Curved openings in stone, brick or stucco need a door with a matching arched top section. A standard flat-top door leaves an awkward gap or requires covering the arch, which spoils the look. A custom arch solves that problem.

You Need a Specific Color

When the door must match shutters, trim or a front door exactly, a custom paint or stain gets closer than a stock color chart. This matters most on homes where the garage faces the street or water.

Historic Homes

Matching Lake Estates And Detached Carriage Houses

Oconomowoc's lakeshores carry the legacy of the era when wealthy Chicago and Milwaukee families built summer estates here, earning the area the name Newport of the West. Many of those properties include detached carriage houses and garages built long before standard door sizes existed. Their openings can be unusually wide, oddly tall or slightly out of square, and the original doors were often swing-out wood doors. A modern overhead door that looks like the original is often the best of both worlds. It keeps the look while adding modern convenience.

For these buildings, we look closely at the existing trim, siding, roof pitch and any original hardware or details that can guide the design. A custom carriage house door with board patterns, window layouts and hardware drawn from the house itself keeps the building looking like it belongs on the property. In some cases the door may need to fit an arched opening or sit within a stone or brick surround that cannot be changed. Custom sizing and arched top sections make that possible without altering the historic structure. The result looks original.

The same thinking applies to historic homes in downtown neighborhoods near Fowler Lake and Lac La Belle, where an alley or side-street garage is part of a block with real character. A plain stock door can look out of place there, and a custom design keeps the streetscape intact. We take time to study the neighboring homes as well. Matching the scale, trim and paint traditions of the block helps a new door feel like it has always been there, and it keeps these historic streets looking their best.

Planning the Project

How We Plan and Install A Custom Garage Door

Every custom project starts with a conversation about how you use the garage and what you want the door to do for the house. From there we measure, photograph the opening and sketch out options for size, material, glass and color. We keep the choices manageable and explain the practical side of each one, including weight, insulation and upkeep. When the design is set, we give you a written quote that covers the door, the hardware, the springs, any opener changes and the installation. We quote the price before work starts.

On installation day, our technicians remove the old door, prepare the opening, set the tracks and springs for the new door's weight, and hang the sections. We balance the door carefully, since a well-balanced custom door is easier on the opener and safer to use. We test the opener, the auto-reverse and the photo-eye sensors, and we show you how everything works before we leave. If something about the opening needs a carpenter or mason first, we tell you early so the schedule stays on track. A well-planned install is what makes a custom door last.

Custom Garage Doors FAQ

Questions About Custom Garage Doors

Answers from the technicians who do this work across Oconomowoc every week.

Ask A Technician
01How tall can a custom garage door be?

Custom doors can be built well beyond the standard seven or eight foot heights, with ten, twelve and even taller doors common for boats, campers and car lifts. The practical limit is usually the height of your garage walls and ceiling and the track setup. We measure your space and explain which heights and lift styles will work before you choose a size.

02Are real wood doors practical in Wisconsin?

They can be, with the right expectations. Cedar and mahogany hold up well when they are finished properly and resealed on a regular schedule. Snow, salt and humid summers will wear a neglected finish quickly. If you love the look but want less upkeep, wood composite or woodgrain steel doors are good alternatives, and we can show you both side by side.

03Can you match a door to my historic carriage house?

Yes. We look at the trim, siding, roof, windows and any original hardware, and use those details to guide the board pattern, window layout and finish of the new door. Many historic Oconomowoc carriage houses had swing-out doors, and a custom overhead door can recreate that look while opening like a modern door. We match colors and hardware as well.

04Do custom doors take longer to get?

Yes, because they are built to order. Lead times depend on the size, material and the manufacturer's schedule, and wood or specialty glass doors typically take longer than custom-sized steel doors. We give you a realistic timeline with your quote. If your current door is failing, we can repair it to keep the garage usable until the new door is ready.

05Will I need a new opener for a custom door?

Often, yes. Tall doors and real wood doors are heavier than standard doors, and high lift tracks change how the door travels. A wall-mounted jackshaft opener is a common choice because it handles heavier doors and frees the ceiling. We check your current opener and tell you plainly whether it can do the job or should be replaced. It is part of the quote.

06Can a custom door fit an arched opening?

Yes. Doors can be built with an arched top section that follows the curve of the opening, which is common on older brick, stone and stucco homes. The arch must be measured carefully, since older openings are not always even. We take precise measurements so the door fills the arch cleanly without gaps or awkward trim. The finished look is clean and intentional.
