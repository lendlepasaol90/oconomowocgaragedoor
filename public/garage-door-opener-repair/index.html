Finding the Cause

Is It the Opener Or Is It the Door

A lot of so-called opener problems are really door problems. If a torsion spring has broken, the door can weigh well over a hundred pounds of dead weight, and the opener will strain, hum or reverse because it was never meant to lift that load. The same goes for a frayed cable, a bent track or seized rollers. Replacing the opener in those cases does not fix anything, and the new opener wears out early.

That is why we start every opener call by checking the door. We disconnect the opener and look at how the door moves by hand, whether it stays put halfway up, and whether the springs, cables, rollers and tracks are in good shape. If the door is the problem, we tell you, and we do not charge you for an opener part you do not need. Please do not try to adjust springs or cables yourself. They are under high tension and can cause serious injury.

If the door checks out, we move to the opener: power, wall button, remotes, sensors, the drive and gears, then the logic board and settings. Working in that order finds the real fault quickly and keeps the repair focused.

Common Repairs

What Usually Goes Wrong Inside an Opener

Most opener failures fall into a handful of categories. On many chain and belt openers, the motor turns a worm gear that drives a larger plastic gear. Over years of use, especially if the door has been out of balance, that gear wears and strips. The telltale sign is a motor that runs and hums while the chain or belt does not move. A gear kit replacement often brings a solid opener back to life.

Logic boards are the opener's brain. They read the remote signals, the wall button, the sensors and the limit switches, and they tell the motor what to do. Power surges and lightning storms are a common cause of board failure. Symptoms include no response at all, lights that blink but no movement, or erratic behavior. Boards can often be replaced, depending on the model and parts availability.

Other common repairs include worn chains or belts that have stretched or slipped, broken trolley carriages, failed capacitors that keep the motor from starting, bad wall buttons and damaged wiring. We carry many common parts on the truck so we can finish the repair on the first visit whenever possible.

  • Stripped drive gear and sprocket
  • Failed logic board after a power surge
  • Misaligned or damaged photo-eye sensors
  • Stretched chain or worn belt
  • Broken trolley or carriage
  • Bad wall button or wiring
  • Remotes and keypads that stopped responding
  • Limit and force settings out of adjustment

Sensors and Settings

Photo-Eyes, Limits And Force Adjustments

If your door starts to close and then reverses, or only closes when you hold the wall button down, the photo-eye sensors are the first suspect. These small units near the floor must see each other's beam. A bumped bracket, a spider web, a muddy lens or bright low sun shining straight into one eye can block the signal. Wiring nicked by a shovel or chewed by a mouse causes the same symptom. Most sensor problems are quick to fix once we track down the cause.

Limit settings tell the opener where to stop at the top and bottom. If the down limit is wrong, the door may stop short and leave a gap, or push too hard into the floor and reverse. Force settings control how hard the opener pushes before deciding something is in the way. In cold weather the door gets stiffer, and a force setting that was fine in July can cause reversals in January.

We adjust limits and force carefully and always finish with a safety reversal test. Turning the force way up to stop reversals is not a safe fix. It can defeat the reversal feature and hides an underlying door problem that should be addressed instead.

What To Look For

Signs Your Opener Needs Repair

Get A Free Quote

Motor Hums, Door Stays Put

If you hear the motor running but the chain or belt is not moving, the drive gear is often stripped. It can also mean the door is disconnected from the trolley, or a broken spring is making the door too heavy.

Door Reverses Before Closing

A door that starts down and pops back up usually points to blocked or misaligned photo-eyes. It can also be a force setting issue, a down limit set too low, or a door that binds somewhere along the track.

Remote Works Only Up Close

Short remote range can come from a weak battery, a damaged antenna wire, or radio interference from LED bulbs in the opener. If the wall button works fine, the opener's motor and drive are probably healthy.

Blinking Lights, No Movement

Many openers blink their lights in a pattern to report a fault, often related to the sensors or the logic board. The pattern means something specific for each model and helps narrow down the repair.

Grinding or Clicking Noises

New grinding, clicking or popping from the opener can mean worn gears, a loose chain, a damaged trolley or a sprocket starting to fail. Catching it early can prevent a full breakdown on a cold morning.

Opener Dead After a Storm

If the opener stopped working after a thunderstorm or power flicker, a surge may have damaged the logic board. Check the outlet and breaker first. If those are fine, the board may need replacing.

Remotes and Keypads

When the Clicker Stops Clicking

A remote that stops working is often just a battery. If a fresh battery does not fix it, the remote may have lost its programming, or the opener's receiver may have a problem. When one remote works and another does not, the issue is the remote. When none work but the wall button does, the issue is in the opener or its antenna. A dangling antenna wire that has been pushed up or damaged can sharply reduce range.

Keypads have the same battery issue, plus exposure to weather. Water can get into an older keypad, and cold can kill a weak battery fast. Sometimes the keypad simply loses its code. We reprogram remotes and keypads, replace units that have failed, and check that your vehicle's built-in controls are still paired with the opener.

Range problems can also come from interference. LED bulbs in the opener's light sockets are a frequent culprit, since some models put out radio noise that drowns out the remote signal. Swapping to a bulb rated for garage door openers often fixes a remote that only works from a few feet away.

Repair or Replace

When a Repair Makes Sense And When It Does Not

We give you a straight answer on repair versus replacement. A newer opener with a stripped gear, a bad sensor or a misprogrammed remote is almost always worth repairing. The parts are available and the rest of the unit has plenty of life left. A single failed board on a mid-life opener is often worth fixing too, depending on the price of the part.

Replacement starts to make sense when the opener is very old, when parts are no longer made for it, or when the repair would cost a large share of a new unit. Very old openers may also lack modern safety sensors, rolling-code security and features like battery backup. If the opener is loud and you have a bedroom over the garage, that is another good reason to consider a quieter belt or wall-mount model.

We will give you the price of the repair and, if it is close, the price of a replacement, and let you decide. There is no pressure either way. Plenty of homeowners in Oconomowoc keep a solid older opener running for years with the right repair.

Local Know-How

Why Oconomowoc Openers Act Up in Winter

Opener calls in Oconomowoc pick up when the temperature drops. Cold thickens grease, stiffens weather seals and makes doors heavier to move, so an opener that worked fine in October may start reversing in January. Frost and ice can freeze the bottom seal to the floor, and an opener that keeps pulling against it can strip its gear. Summer brings its own trouble, with thunderstorms over the lakes causing surges that damage logic boards. Lake humidity adds condensation that can corrode keypad contacts and sensor wiring. Our repairs account for the season, from checking force settings in cold weather to looking for moisture damage in older detached garages.

Opener Repair FAQ

Questions About Opener Repair

Answers from the technicians who do this work across Oconomowoc every week.

Ask A Technician
01Why does my garage door go up but not down?

The most common reason is a problem with the photo-eye sensors. If they are blocked, dirty, misaligned or have a damaged wire, the opener will refuse to close the door with the remote. Many openers will still close if you hold the wall button down. Check that nothing is in the beam and the lenses are clean. If it continues, we can find and fix the cause.

02My opener hums but nothing moves. What is wrong?

Often the drive gear is stripped, so the motor spins without moving the chain or belt. It can also be a failed capacitor, a disconnected trolley, or a broken spring making the door too heavy to lift. Do not keep pressing the button, since that can burn out the motor. Call us and we will check the door and opener.

03Is it worth repairing an old garage door opener?

It depends on age, condition and parts availability. A solid opener with a stripped gear or bad sensor is usually worth repairing. A very old opener with no safety sensors, no rolling-code security or hard-to-find parts may be better replaced. We quote the repair and, when it is close, the replacement too, so you can make the decision with real numbers.

04Why does my door reverse more in cold weather?

Cold makes the door stiffer and seals less flexible, so the opener has to push harder. If the force setting is near its limit, the opener may decide something is in the way and reverse. Ice at the bottom seal can do the same. The answer is a properly balanced door and correct settings, not cranking the force up to its maximum.

05Can you program new remotes and keypads?

Yes. We can program new or replacement remotes and keypads, reset codes, and pair compatible vehicle controls with your opener. If an older opener uses a radio system that newer remotes no longer support, we will explain your options, which may include a receiver add-on or a new opener. We test each remote before we leave.

06Should I unplug my opener during a thunderstorm?

Unplugging during a severe storm can protect the logic board from surges, and a surge protector rated for openers adds protection all the time. If your opener stopped working after a storm, check the outlet, the breaker and any GFCI reset first. If power is reaching the opener and it still does nothing, the board may be damaged and need replacing.
