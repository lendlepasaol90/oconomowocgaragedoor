The Core Styles

Six Garage Door Styles And What Sets Each Apart

Raised panel doors are the most common style on Oconomowoc streets, and for good reason. The panels are pressed outward from the face of each section, which creates shadow lines that look tidy on almost any house. Short panels give a traditional, busy rhythm. Long panels stretch horizontally and suit ranch homes and wider two-car doors. Recessed panel doors flip the idea, setting the panels back into the frame for a flatter, cleaner surface. Shaker doors are a version of the recessed style with simple square edges and wide stiles, borrowed from the cabinet world and popular on newer farmhouse and transitional homes.

Carriage house doors are built to look like the old swing-out barn and coach doors that sat on estate outbuildings. They still roll up overhead like any sectional door, but the face is designed with vertical boards, cross bucks or a center seam, often with decorative handles and strap hinges. Modern flush doors go the other way, with smooth or lightly grooved faces and no raised detail at all. They read clean and horizontal, and they pair well with dark colors, metal finishes and contemporary rooflines. Full-view doors are mostly glass set into aluminum frames and turn the garage into a bright room when the door is closed.

Every one of these styles comes in a range of materials and insulation levels, so picking a look does not lock you into a single price or performance level. A raised panel door can be a single layer of steel or a thick insulated sandwich. A carriage house look can be real wood, wood composite or embossed steel. We keep style and construction as two separate conversations so you can see the tradeoffs clearly. That way, the design you like and the performance you need both get proper attention, and the final quote reflects the exact door you chose.

  • Raised panel, short or long
  • Recessed panel and shaker
  • Carriage house with or without overlays
  • Modern flush and grooved
  • Full-view glass and aluminum
  • Hybrid designs with windows on top

Windows and Hardware

The Details That Finish the Look

Windows change a door more than most people expect. A single row of windows across the top section lets light into the garage while keeping the lower sections private and solid. Arched window inserts soften a traditional door. Square and long rectangular windows work on carriage house and shaker designs. On modern doors, a narrow band of frosted or tinted glass can carry the horizontal lines of the house across the garage. Glass options include clear, frosted, obscure and tinted, and insulated glass is worth considering if you heat the garage or have living space above it.

Decorative hardware is the other finishing touch. Handles and hinge straps in black or bronze finishes make a carriage house door look like it could swing open. On a raised panel door, the same hardware can look busy, so we usually suggest using it lightly or skipping it. Color matters too. A door painted to match the siding fades into the house, while a door that matches the trim or the front door becomes a feature. Woodgrain finishes on steel give warmth without the upkeep of real wood, and darker colors suit flush doors very well. We bring samples so you can see them against your actual siding and brick. Small choices like these make a big difference once the door is on the house.

  • Top-row windows for light and privacy
  • Arched, square and rectangular inserts
  • Clear, frosted, obscure or tinted glass
  • Handles and strap hinges in dark finishes
  • Woodgrain and solid color options

Local Architecture

Matching Styles to Oconomowoc Homes

Oconomowoc has a wider range of house styles than most towns its size. Near Lac La Belle and Fowler Lake you will find older homes with steep roofs, wood siding and porches, and many of them have detached garages tucked behind. Carriage house doors in painted finishes or woodgrain fit these homes well because they echo the outbuildings that once stood on the same lots. On the historic lake estates, the original garages were often coach houses, and a well-chosen carriage door keeps that character intact. Simple trim colors usually look best.

The 1970s through 1990s subdivisions around the city tend to have attached two-car and three-car garages on ranch, split-level and two-story colonial homes. A long or short raised panel door is the safe pick here, but a recessed panel or shaker door with a top row of windows can update the front of these houses without fighting the original design. At Pabst Farms, many newer homes have craftsman, farmhouse or transitional exteriors with stone accents and three-car garages. Shaker, carriage house and modern flush doors all show up in that neighborhood, and taller doors are common.

Lakefront homes deserve a little extra thought because they are often seen from two sides, the road and the water. If your garage faces the lake or a neighbor's dock, the door is part of the view, and many owners on Oconomowoc Lake and Okauchee Lake choose quieter colors and simpler designs that sit back into the landscape. Earth tones, soft grays and woodgrain finishes tend to blend with trees and shoreline. A bold color can work, too, but it is worth seeing a sample from the dock as well as from the driveway before you decide. We are happy to walk the property with you.

What To Look For

Signs It Is Time For a New Door Style

Get A Free Quote

The Door Looks Dated

If the rest of the house has new siding, windows or a new roof and the garage door is the oldest thing on the front, it pulls the whole exterior back a few decades. A new style can make the house look finished again.

Panels Are Dented or Cracked

Thin steel doors dent easily from bikes, balls and snow blowers, and older wood panels crack and split after years of freeze-thaw cycles. Once several sections are damaged, replacing the whole door is often more sensible than patching.

The Garage Is Too Dark

A solid door with no windows makes the garage feel like a cave. Adding a row of windows or choosing a glass style brings daylight into a workshop or play space without opening the door.

You Are Preparing to Sell

Buyers form an opinion before they walk in. A clean, well-matched garage door is one of the most visible updates you can make to the front of a home, and it photographs well in a listing.

Finish Is Peeling or Faded

Sun, salt and humid lake summers wear down paint and stain. When the finish is chalky, peeling or rusting at the bottom edge, it is a good time to consider whether a different style would serve the house better.

Style Clashes With the House

Sometimes a past owner chose a door that never matched the architecture, like a plain flat door on a historic cottage. Picking a style that fits the home's era makes the whole property look more intentional.

Style and Climate

Choosing a Style That Handles Wisconsin Weather

Style and weather are linked more than you might think. Lake Country gets around 40 inches of snow a year and has seen temperatures down to 33 below zero. A door with deep raised panels and lots of edges can collect ice and road slush at the bottom. A flush door sheds water more easily but can show dents and dings in a busy driveway. Full-view glass doors look great, but unless you choose insulated glass and frames, they let a lot of cold into the garage during January. Every style has a version that works here.

Dark colors on steel doors absorb more heat in summer, and in humid lake weather that can be hard on finishes over time. Lighter colors and quality factory paint systems hold up best on sunny south and west facing garages. Wood carriage doors look wonderful but need regular sealing to keep moisture and salt spray from soaking in. We walk through these practical points with you so the door you love looking at is also a door you will be happy living with for years. None of these concerns rule out any style. They just shape which version of that style makes sense.

Our Approach

How We Help You Pick the Right Door

Choosing from a catalog is hard because a small picture never looks like your house. When we visit, we look at the roofline, the siding, the trim color, the front door and the windows, and we take measurements of the opening, the headroom and the side room. From there we narrow the field to a few styles that suit the home and fit the space. You see samples in daylight against your own exterior, not under showroom lights. We also ask how you use the garage, since a workshop, a gym or a boat bay can change which style makes the most sense.

We also check the parts of the system that a new door depends on. Springs, tracks, the opener and the framing around the opening all need to suit the new door's weight and size. A heavier insulated or wood door may need different springs or a stronger opener, and we tell you that up front. We quote the price before any work starts, and when you are ready to order, we handle the install so the new style looks right and runs smoothly from the first day. Your new door should look good on day one and for years after.

Garage Door Styles FAQ

Questions About Garage Door Styles

Answers from the technicians who do this work across Oconomowoc every week.

Ask A Technician
01What is the most popular garage door style in Oconomowoc?

Raised panel doors are still the most common because they suit so many house types and come in many price points. That said, carriage house and shaker styles have become very popular on newer homes and on older homes getting exterior updates. On the lakes and in older neighborhoods, carriage house doors are a frequent pick because they match the outbuildings and estate garages that give the area its character.

02Can I get a carriage house look without real wood?

Yes. Many carriage house doors are made from insulated steel with a woodgrain texture, or from wood composite overlays on a steel base. These give the look of board-and-batten or cross buck doors with far less maintenance than real wood. They still open overhead like a standard sectional door, and the decorative handles and hinges are added for appearance only. We can show samples of both.

03Do windows make a garage door colder?

Windows lose more heat than an insulated steel panel, but the difference depends on the glass. Single pane glass lets in a lot of cold. Insulated, double pane glass performs much better and is the right choice if you heat the garage or have rooms above it. Keeping windows to the top section limits heat loss while still adding light and a nicer look from the street.

04Will a new style fit my existing opening?

Most styles come in standard widths and heights that fit typical openings, so a new look usually fits without framing changes. We measure the opening, the headroom above it and the room on each side before quoting. If your opening is unusual, as is common on older detached garages near the lakes, we can look at custom sizes or adjust the framing as part of the job.

05How do I choose a color that matches my house?

Start by deciding whether you want the door to blend in or stand out. Matching the siding makes the door recede, while matching the trim, shutters or front door makes it a feature. Look at samples outside in daylight, since indoor lighting shifts colors. We bring color chips and woodgrain samples to the visit so you can compare them against your actual house.

06Does a heavier style need a new opener?

Sometimes. Insulated doors, wood doors and doors with lots of glass weigh more than a basic steel door, and the springs must be sized for the new weight. An older opener may struggle even when the springs are correct. We check your current opener during the visit and tell you plainly if it can handle the new door or if an upgrade makes sense.
